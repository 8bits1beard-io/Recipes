# Hot Sausage Spaghetti Sauce

*A recipe by Joshua Walderbach*

Hot ground sausage browned hard for flavor, then simmered into a whole-peeled-tomato sauce with caramelized tomato paste, garlic, oregano, and fresh basil. Finished off the heat with optional butter for a rounder, silkier body.

**Yield:** about 8 servings &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Active time:** ~25 minutes &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Total time:** ~1 hour 20 minutes

---

## Ingredients

- 1 lb (454 g) hot ground sausage
- Up to 1 tbsp (15 mL / ~14 g) olive oil, only if needed after browning the sausage
- 1 medium yellow onion (~1 cup / 5 1/2 oz / 155 g), chopped
- 1 small can (6 oz / 170 g) tomato paste
- 4 garlic cloves (~1 tbsp + 1 tsp / 12–14 g), finely minced
- 1 tsp (~1 g) dried oregano
- Up to 1/4 tsp (~0.5 g) red pepper flakes, optional — the sausage is already hot
- 2 cans whole peeled tomatoes, 28 oz each (56 oz / 1.59 kg total), including juices
- Up to 1 tsp (~6 g) salt, divided and adjusted to taste
- 1/2 tsp (~1 g) black pepper
- 1/2 cup (~20 g) fresh basil, chopped
- 1–2 tbsp (14–28 g) unsalted butter, optional and added only after tasting
- 1/4–1 tsp (~1–4 g) sugar, only if needed

---

## Equipment

- Large saucepan or Dutch oven
- Slotted spoon
- Wooden spoon or potato masher

---

## Instructions

1. **Brown the sausage.** Heat a large saucepan or Dutch oven over **medium-high heat**. Add the hot ground sausage and cook for about **6–8 minutes**, breaking it into small pieces with a wooden spoon or spatula.

   Let the sausage sit against the hot pan for short stretches before stirring so some pieces develop good browning rather than simply steaming. Cook until no pink remains.

2. **Remove the sausage.** Transfer the browned sausage to a bowl with a slotted spoon.

   Leave about **1–2 tablespoons of rendered sausage fat** in the pot and spoon off any excess. If there's less than about **1 tablespoon** of fat in the pot, add up to **1 tbsp (15 mL)** olive oil.

3. **Cook the onion.** Reduce the heat to **medium**. Add the chopped onion and cook, stirring occasionally, until it is **very soft and just beginning to turn lightly golden**, about **5–10 minutes**.

   Because the sausage is already seasoned, do not add extra salt yet.

4. **Cook the tomato paste.** Add the entire **6 oz (170 g)** can of tomato paste. Cook for **2–3 minutes**, stirring frequently and scraping the bottom of the pot. The paste should change from bright red to a noticeably **darker brick-red**.

5. **Bloom the garlic and seasonings.** Add the minced garlic, oregano, and optional red pepper flakes. Cook for **30–45 seconds**, stirring constantly, just until fragrant. Do not let the garlic brown.

6. **Add the tomatoes.** Immediately add both cans of whole peeled tomatoes along with all their juices. Use a wooden spoon or potato masher to break the tomatoes apart to your preferred consistency.

   Scrape the bottom of the pot well so the browned bits and cooked tomato paste dissolve into the sauce.

7. **Return the sausage.** Add the browned sausage and any juices collected in the bowl back to the pot. Stir well so the sausage is evenly distributed through the tomato sauce.

8. **Season carefully.** Add the **1/2 tsp black pepper**. Start with only **1/4–1/2 tsp salt**, or none at all if the sausage tastes particularly salty. You can add more after the sauce has simmered and reduced.

9. **Simmer gently.** Bring the sauce just to a simmer, then reduce the heat enough to maintain **small, gentle bubbles**. Simmer **uncovered for 45–60 minutes**, stirring every **10–15 minutes** and scraping the bottom of the pot.

   The sauce is ready when it has thickened to the consistency you like and the tomato flavor no longer tastes raw.

10. **Add most of the basil.** During the final **3–5 minutes**, stir in about **3/4** of the chopped basil. Reserve the rest.

11. **Finish off the heat.** Turn off the burner and stir in the remaining fresh basil.

    Taste the sauce before adding butter. If you want a rounder, silkier finish, stir in **1 tbsp (14 g)** unsalted butter. Taste again before deciding whether to add the second tablespoon.

12. **Taste and adjust.** Adjust the salt and black pepper as needed.

    If the tomatoes still taste unpleasantly sharp or sour, add **1/4–1/2 tsp sugar**, stir well, and taste again. Add more only if necessary, up to about **1 tsp total**.

---

## To Serve

Cook the spaghetti in well-salted water until it is about **1–2 minutes shy of al dente**. Before draining, save about **1 cup (240 mL)** of pasta water.

Transfer the pasta into a skillet or pot with enough sausage sauce to coat it. Add a small splash of pasta water — start with about **1/4 cup (60 mL)** — and cook everything together over **medium-low heat for 1–2 minutes**, tossing or stirring continuously. Add more pasta water only as needed.

---

## Notes

- Finishing the spaghetti in the sauce lets the starch, tomato sauce, sausage fat, and optional butter form a cohesive coating that clings to the pasta instead of sitting on top of it.
- Darkening the tomato paste before the tomatoes go in builds a deep, roasted backbone that a raw paste can't give you.
- Sugar does not remove acid; it balances how acidic the sauce *tastes*. Reach for salt first.

---

## Swaps & Tips

- Skip the meat entirely and make the [Meatless Spaghetti Sauce](./meatless-spaghetti-sauce.md) instead.
- Sweet Italian sausage works if you want the same body without the heat — add the red pepper flakes back in to compensate.
- Season at the end, not the start. The sauce concentrates as it reduces, and sausage varies widely in salt.

---

## Storage

Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 4–5 days, or freeze for up to 3 months. The flavor deepens overnight.
