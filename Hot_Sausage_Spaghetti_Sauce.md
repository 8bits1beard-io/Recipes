# Hot Sausage Spaghetti Sauce

**Makes:** about 8 servings  
**Simmer time:** about 45–60 minutes  
**Sausage:** 1 lb / 454 g hot ground sausage

## Ingredients

- **Hot ground sausage:** 1 lb | 454 g
- **Olive oil:** up to 1 tablespoon | 15 mL | ~14 g, only if needed after browning the sausage
- **Yellow onion:** 1 medium onion, chopped | about 1 cup | ~5½ oz | ~155 g
- **Tomato paste:** 1 small can | 6 oz | 170 g
- **Fresh garlic:** 4 cloves, finely minced | about 1 tablespoon + 1 teaspoon | ~12–14 g
- **Dried oregano:** 1 teaspoon | ~1 g
- **Red pepper flakes:** ¼ teaspoon | ~0.5 g, optional or reduce to taste since the sausage is already hot
- **Whole peeled tomatoes:** 2 cans, 28 oz each | 56 oz total | 1.59 kg total, including juices
- **Salt:** up to 1 teaspoon | ~6 g, divided and adjusted to taste
- **Black pepper:** ½ teaspoon | ~1 g
- **Fresh basil, chopped:** ½ cup | ~20 g
- **Unsalted butter:** 2 tablespoons | 1 oz | 28 g
- **Sugar, only if needed:** ¼–1 teaspoon | ~1–4 g

## Directions

1. **Brown the sausage.**  
   Heat a large saucepan or Dutch oven over **medium to medium-high heat**.

   Add the hot ground sausage and cook for about **6–8 minutes**, breaking it into small pieces with a wooden spoon or spatula.

   Let the sausage sit against the hot pan for short stretches before stirring so some pieces develop good browning rather than simply steaming.

   Cook until no pink remains.

2. **Manage the rendered fat.**  
   If there is a large amount of grease in the pan, spoon off the excess, leaving about **1–2 tablespoons** behind for flavor.

   If the pan is relatively dry, add up to **1 tablespoon / 15 mL olive oil**.

3. **Cook the onion.**  
   Reduce the heat to **medium**.

   Add the chopped yellow onion. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the onion is **very soft and just beginning to turn lightly golden**, about **5–10 minutes**.

   Because sausage can already contain significant salt, do **not** add extra salt at this stage.

4. **Cook the tomato paste.**  
   Add the entire **6 oz / 170 g can of tomato paste**.

   Cook for **2–3 minutes**, stirring frequently and scraping the bottom of the pan. The paste should change from bright red to a noticeably **darker brick-red**.

5. **Bloom the garlic and seasonings.**  
   Add the minced garlic, oregano, and red pepper flakes.

   Cook for **30–45 seconds**, stirring constantly, just until fragrant. Do not allow the garlic to brown.

   **Note:** Because the sausage is already hot, you can reduce or omit the red pepper flakes if you want a milder finished sauce.

6. **Add the tomatoes.**  
   Immediately add both cans of whole peeled tomatoes along with all their juices.

   Use a wooden spoon or potato masher to break the tomatoes apart to your preferred consistency.

   Scrape the bottom of the pot well so the browned sausage and tomato-paste bits dissolve into the sauce.

7. **Season carefully.**  
   Add the **½ teaspoon black pepper**.

   Start with only **¼–½ teaspoon salt**, or none at all if the sausage tastes particularly salty.

   You can add more after the sauce has simmered and reduced.

8. **Simmer gently.**  
   Bring the sauce just to a simmer, then reduce the heat enough to maintain **small, gentle bubbles**.

   Simmer **uncovered for 45–60 minutes**, stirring every 10–15 minutes and scraping the bottom of the pot.

   The sauce is ready when it has thickened to the consistency you like and the tomato flavor no longer tastes raw.

9. **Add most of the basil.**  
   During the final **3–5 minutes**, stir in about **¾ of the chopped basil**.

   Reserve the rest.

10. **Finish off the heat.**  
    Turn off the burner.

    Stir in the remaining fresh basil and **2 tablespoons / 28 g unsalted butter** until the butter is completely melted and incorporated.

11. **Taste and adjust.**  
    Taste the finished sauce before adding any more salt.

    Adjust the salt and black pepper as needed.

    If the tomatoes still taste unpleasantly sharp or sour, add **¼–½ teaspoon sugar**, stir well, and taste again. Add more only if necessary, up to about **1 teaspoon total**.

    The sugar does not remove the acid; it balances how acidic the sauce tastes.

## For the Best Spaghetti

Cook the spaghetti in well-salted water until it is about **1–2 minutes shy of al dente**.

Before draining, save about **1 cup / 240 mL pasta water**.

Transfer the pasta into a skillet or pot with enough sausage sauce to coat it. Add a small splash of pasta water—start with about **¼ cup / 60 mL**—and cook everything together over **medium-low heat for 1–2 minutes**, tossing or stirring continuously.

Add more pasta water only as needed.

Finishing the spaghetti in the sauce helps the starch, tomato sauce, sausage fat, and butter form a cohesive coating that clings to the pasta.
