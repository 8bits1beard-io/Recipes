# Meatless Spaghetti Sauce

*A recipe by Joshua Walderbach*

The same slow-simmered tomato sauce without the meat — olive oil, onion, darkened tomato paste, garlic, red pepper flakes, and fresh basil, finished off the heat with butter.

**Yield:** about 6–8 servings &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Active time:** ~20 minutes &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Total time:** ~1 hour 15 minutes

---

## Ingredients

- 2 tbsp (30 mL / ~27 g) olive oil
- 1 medium yellow onion (~1 cup / 5 1/2 oz / 155 g), chopped
- 1 small can (6 oz / 170 g) tomato paste
- 4 garlic cloves (~1 tbsp + 1 tsp / 12–14 g), finely minced
- 1 tsp (~1 g) dried oregano
- 1/4 tsp (~0.5 g) red pepper flakes
- 2 cans whole peeled tomatoes, 28 oz each (56 oz / 1.59 kg total), including juices
- About 1 tsp (~6 g) salt, divided and adjusted to taste
- 1/2 tsp (~1 g) black pepper
- 1/2 cup (~20 g) fresh basil, chopped
- 2 tbsp (1 oz / 28 g) unsalted butter
- 1/4–1 tsp (~1–4 g) sugar, only if needed

---

## Equipment

- Large saucepan or Dutch oven
- Wooden spoon or potato masher

---

## Instructions

1. **Cook the onion.** Heat the olive oil in a large saucepan or Dutch oven over **medium heat**. Add the chopped onion and a small pinch of the measured salt.

   Cook, stirring occasionally, until the onion is **very soft and just beginning to turn lightly golden**, usually about **5–10 minutes**.

2. **Cook the tomato paste.** Add the entire **6 oz (170 g)** can of tomato paste. Cook for **2–3 minutes**, stirring frequently and scraping the bottom of the pan. The paste should change from bright red to a noticeably **darker brick-red**.

3. **Bloom the garlic and seasonings.** Add the minced garlic, oregano, and red pepper flakes. Cook for only **30–45 seconds**, stirring constantly, until the garlic becomes fragrant. Do not let the garlic brown.

4. **Add the tomatoes.** Immediately add both cans of whole peeled tomatoes along with all their juices. Use a wooden spoon or potato masher to break the tomatoes apart — leave them somewhat chunky or crush them thoroughly depending on the texture you prefer.

5. **Season.** Add about **3/4 tsp of the salt** and the **1/2 tsp black pepper**.

   Save the remaining salt for final adjustment, because the sauce will become more concentrated as it reduces.

6. **Simmer gently.** Bring the sauce just to a simmer, then reduce the heat enough to maintain **small, gentle bubbles**. Simmer **uncovered for 45–60 minutes**, stirring every **10–15 minutes** and scraping the bottom of the pan.

   Don't worry about hitting an exact time. The sauce is ready when it has thickened to the consistency you like and the tomatoes no longer taste raw.

7. **Add most of the basil.** During the final **3–5 minutes**, stir in about **3/4** of the chopped basil. Reserve the rest.

8. **Finish off the heat.** Turn off the burner. Add the remaining fresh basil and **2 tbsp (28 g)** unsalted butter. Stir until the butter is completely melted and incorporated into the sauce.

9. **Taste before adding sugar.** Taste the finished sauce and first adjust the **salt and black pepper** if necessary.

   If the tomatoes still taste unpleasantly sharp or sour, add **1/4–1/2 tsp sugar**, stir well, and taste again. Add more only if needed, up to about **1 tsp total**.

---

## To Serve

Cook the spaghetti in well-salted water until it's about **1–2 minutes shy of al dente**. Before draining, save about **1 cup (240 mL)** of pasta water.

Transfer the pasta into a skillet or pot with enough sauce to coat it. Add a small splash of pasta water — start with about **1/4 cup (60 mL)** — and cook everything together over **medium-low heat for 1–2 minutes**, tossing or stirring continuously. Add a little more pasta water only if needed.

---

## Notes

- That final toss in the pan is what makes the sauce **cling to the spaghetti instead of simply sitting on top of it**.
- Darkening the tomato paste before the tomatoes go in builds a deep, roasted backbone that a raw paste can't give you.
- The butter goes in off the heat so it emulsifies into the sauce rather than splitting.
- Sugar isn't removing the acid; it's balancing how acidic the sauce *tastes*.

---

## Swaps & Tips

- Want it meaty? Make the [Hot Sausage Spaghetti Sauce](./hot-sausage-spaghetti-sauce.md) instead.
- Season at the end, not the start — the sauce concentrates considerably as it reduces.
- A parmesan rind dropped in during the simmer adds savory depth (no longer strictly meatless-vegan, but excellent).

---

## Storage

Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 4–5 days, or freeze for up to 3 months. The flavor deepens overnight.
